## Why There Is No One-Size Price

Permanent lighting is custom-fit to each home. The track is measured, cut and mounted to follow your specific roofline, parapet, fence or patio. Controllers and power are planned around your electrical setup. Because every install is built for the house it is on, the cost reflects the work your home actually needs.

That is also why we start with a site visit. Measuring in person lets us give you a clear, itemized estimate instead of a rough guess.

## The Main Factors That Affect Cost

### Linear Footage

The biggest single factor is how many feet of lighting your project includes. A compact single-story home with a simple front roofline needs much less track and fewer lights than a large two-story home lit on all four sides.

Many homeowners start with the front of the house, since that is what you and your neighbors see most. Others light the full perimeter from the start. Either approach works, and you can always expand later.

### Roofline Complexity

A long, straight run of fascia is faster to install than a roofline with many corners, peaks, gables and changes in direction. Each transition takes extra measuring, cutting and fitting so the lights flow cleanly around the shape of the house.

In Albuquerque, we see a wide range of rooflines. Simple ranch homes in neighborhoods like the Northeast Heights tend to have clean, continuous lines. Newer two-story stucco homes often have multiple roof planes and gables. Northern New Mexico-style homes with pitched metal roofs have their own considerations.

### Number of Stories

Height affects both time and equipment. Second-story rooflines require more setup and care to install safely. A two-story home will generally take longer than a single-story home with the same linear footage.

### Parapet vs. Pitched Roof

Flat-roof homes with parapets are common across the metro, especially Pueblo Revival and Territorial styles. Mounting on a parapet means working with stucco, planning around canales and drainage, and sealing each mounting point carefully. That is different from mounting to a fascia board on a pitched roof, and it can affect the scope of the job.

### Controllers and Zones

The controller is the brain of the system. It connects the lights to the app on your phone. Larger homes or more complex layouts may need more than one controller.

Zones let you control different sections independently. For example, you might want the front roofline on one zone and the back patio on another, so you can run warm white in the backyard while the front shows holiday colors. More zones add flexibility and can add to the cost.

### Power Access

Every system needs power. If there is a convenient outlet near where the controller will go, such as in the garage or under the eave, the setup is straightforward. If power needs to be brought to a new location, that adds to the project. We look at this during the site visit so there are no surprises later.

### Add-Ons Beyond the Roofline

Many homeowners extend their system beyond the main roofline. Common additions include:

- **Fence and wall lighting** along CMU block walls or courtyard walls
- **Landscape lighting** for xeriscape beds, trees and pathways
- **Patio and portal lighting** for outdoor living areas, ramadas and pergolas
- **Security accent lighting** around entries, garages and side yards

Each of these adds scope, but they can also be phased in over time.

## Factors That Affect Value, Not Just Cost

When you compare quotes, it helps to look past the bottom line and consider what you are getting.

**Materials built for the high desert.** Intense UV, spring wind, monsoon rain and freeze-thaw nights are hard on outdoor equipment. A system designed for those conditions is worth more than one that is not.

**Installation quality.** Clean track runs, careful sealing on stucco, and attention to drainage all affect how the system looks and how long it lasts.

**Track that blends in.** A color-matched, low-profile track that is nearly invisible during the day keeps your home looking good year-round.

**Local support.** A local crew that can come back if you ever need help, plus a system backed by our local warranty, gives you peace of mind. See our warranty page for details.

**Year-round use.** Permanent lighting is not just for Christmas. Warm white for everyday evenings, plus colors for every holiday, Balloon Fiesta, Lobos game days and more, all from one app.

## Planning Tips to Fit Your Budget

- **Start with the front.** Lighting the street-facing roofline first gives you the biggest visual impact. You can add the sides and back later.
- **Prioritize what you use most.** If you spend summer evenings on the back portal, that might be a better first phase than the side of the garage.
- **Think about the long term.** Compare the one-time cost against years of seasonal installs, replacement strands and ladder time.
