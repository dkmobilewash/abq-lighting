Many homes across the Albuquerque metro sit in planned communities with a homeowners association. If yours does, you may be wondering whether permanent lighting is allowed, and what you need to do before installation day.

Every HOA is different, and we cannot tell you what your specific community will or will not approve. What we can do is walk you through a practical process that makes the review smoother, and explain how we help prepare the design information your architectural committee is likely to ask for.

## Why HOAs Care About Exterior Lighting

Most HOAs exist in part to keep a consistent look across the neighborhood. Changes to the outside of a home, including paint colors, fences, landscaping and lighting, often go through some kind of review. That is not a bad thing. It just means a little preparation goes a long way.

When it comes to lighting, committees often think about things like how visible the hardware is during the day, how bright the lights are at night, whether colored or animated displays are allowed and when, and whether lighting spills onto neighboring properties. A clear, complete submission that answers these questions up front usually makes the conversation easier.

## Step 1: Read Your CC&Rs and Design Guidelines

Start with your community's governing documents. The CC&Rs, or Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions, set the ground rules. Many communities also have separate architectural or design guidelines that go into more detail about exterior changes.

Look for sections that mention:

- Exterior lighting or light fixtures
- Holiday decorations and how long they can stay up
- Exterior modifications or alterations
- The architectural review process and required forms
- Any rules about colors, brightness or timing

If you cannot find your documents, your HOA management company or board can usually provide them. Read carefully, because the wording matters. Some guidelines treat permanent lighting as an exterior fixture, while others focus on holiday displays. Knowing which applies helps you frame your request.

## Step 2: Find Out How Architectural Review Works

Most HOAs that review exterior changes have an architectural review committee, sometimes called an ARC, a design review committee, or something similar. Before you submit, find out:

- Whether there is a specific application form
- How submissions are delivered, whether by email, an online portal or paper
- How often the committee meets and how long a decision typically takes
- Whether neighbor signatures or notifications are required

Plan ahead. If you want lights up for the holidays, submitting early in the fall gives you time for questions or revisions before the busy season.

## Step 3: Gather What to Include in Your Submission

A strong submission answers questions before the committee has to ask them. Here is what we recommend including.

### Photos of Your Home

Include clear daytime photos of each side of the house where lighting will go. Mark up the photos to show exactly where the track will run, such as along the fascia, under the eaves, or just below a parapet cap.

### Track Color and Profile

One of the biggest concerns is how the hardware will look during the day. Our system uses a low-profile track, and we help you choose a color that blends with your fascia, trim or stucco. Including the track color and a description of its slim profile shows the committee that the system is designed to be nearly invisible by day.

### Mounting Location and Method

Describe where and how the track mounts. On pitched roofs, it usually attaches to the fascia or under the drip edge. On flat-roof or parapet homes, it typically mounts just below the cap or along a trim band. Explaining that the installation respects stucco and drainage can address concerns about damage to the home.

### Lighting Schedule

Committees often want to know when the lights will be on. A sample schedule helps. For example: warm white from sunset to a set evening hour for everyday accent lighting, with colored scenes limited to recognized holidays and special occasions. Because the system is app-controlled, you can commit to a schedule and follow it consistently.

### Brightness and Effects

Explain that brightness is adjustable from the app and that you plan to keep it at a level appropriate for the neighborhood. If your community has preferences about animated effects, note that you can use steady colors or gentle fades instead of fast chases, or limit effects to certain dates.

### Product Information

A basic spec sheet with the light type, track dimensions and finish options gives the committee something concrete to review.

## Step 4: Submit, Follow Up and Adjust if Needed

Once your submission is in, stay available for questions. Sometimes a committee will ask for a different track color, a shorter run, or a narrower schedule. Small adjustments are usually easy with permanent lighting, because so much is controlled through the app rather than the hardware.

Keep a copy of your approval and any conditions with your home records. If you ever sell, it is helpful for the next owner to know the lighting was reviewed.

## Tips for a Neighbor-Friendly Lighting Plan

Even without HOA rules, it is good practice to be considerate. A few ideas:

- **Lead with warm white.** Everyday warm white accent lighting tends to fit any neighborhood and looks great on stucco homes.
- **Save colors for occasions.** Holidays, Balloon Fiesta week or a big game are natural times for color.
- **Use a sensible shut-off time.** Scheduling lights to turn off at a reasonable hour is an easy way to be a good neighbor.
- **Aim light at your home, not theirs.** Roofline lighting naturally outlines your house rather than shining across the street.
