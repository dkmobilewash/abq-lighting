Every November, a lot of Albuquerque households run the same routine. Someone drags the ladder out of the garage, untangles last year's strands on the driveway, discovers which ones stopped working, and spends a weekend clipping lights to the gutters. Then in January, the whole thing happens again in reverse.

Permanent lighting changes that routine completely. But it is a real investment, and the honest question is whether it makes sense for your home over the long run. Instead of throwing numbers at you, this guide walks through the cost categories that actually matter over five holiday seasons and gives you a simple framework to fill in with your own figures.

## Why Look at Five Years Instead of One?

If you compare a single season of traditional lights against a one-time permanent install, traditional lights will almost always look cheaper. But traditional lights are a recurring cost, paid in money or time every year.

Permanent lighting front-loads the cost into one professional install, and then the lights stay up year-round. Looking across five seasons puts both approaches on equal footing.

## The Cost Categories for Traditional Christmas Lights

### Installation and Removal Labor

There are really two versions of this cost. If you hire a seasonal installer, you pay for a crew to hang lights every fall and take them down every winter. That is two visits a year, every year, and the bill does not go away after the first season.

If you do it yourself, the cost is your time. Count the hours honestly: hauling out bins, testing strands, hanging, adjusting, and then removing and packing everything in January. Many homeowners find it adds up to more than a full weekend each season, often at a time of year when the weekends are already crowded.

### Ladder Risk

This is the cost nobody likes to write down. Climbing onto a two-story stucco home in Rio Rancho or a pitched metal roof in the East Mountains is not a casual job, especially on a cold, breezy late-November afternoon. A fall, a sprained wrist or a cracked roof tile is a real cost, even if it never shows up on a receipt.

You do not need to put a number on it, but it belongs in the comparison.

### Replacement Strands and Clips

High-desert sun is hard on seasonal lights. Intense UV at our elevation can make plastic wire and clips brittle, and strands that spend time in a hot garage or shed all summer do not always come back to life in November. Most families end up buying at least some replacement strands, bulbs or clips each year.

### Storage

Seasonal lights take up space for the eleven months they are not in use. That might be shelf space in the garage, a spot in a storage unit, or simply the frustration of digging through tangled bins.

### Power

Older incandescent strands draw noticeably more power than modern LEDs. If your traditional setup is a mix of old and new strands, your electric bill reflects that during the season. Permanent systems use LEDs and can be scheduled, so they only run when you want them to.

### Repairs and Gutter or Fascia Wear

Clipping and unclipping lights every year can wear on gutters, shingles and stucco edges, and old staples or nails sometimes leave damage behind.

## The Cost Categories for Permanent Lighting

### The One-Time Install

This is the main cost. It depends on factors such as your linear footage, how complex your roofline is, the number of stories, whether you have a pitched roof or a parapet, how many zones and controllers you need, and where power is available. We cover these factors in more detail on our [permanent Christmas lights page](/permanent-holiday-lights/christmas-lights/).

### Ongoing Upkeep

Permanent lighting is not literally zero-maintenance, but upkeep is minimal. The low-profile track is designed to stay in place through sun, wind and monsoon storms, and it is backed by our local warranty. If something ever needs attention, a local crew handles it.

### Power

The system uses LEDs and runs on a schedule you control from the app. Warm white for everyday evenings, colors for occasions, and off whenever you like.

### Your Time

After installation, your time cost is close to nothing. You choose a scene on your phone, set a schedule once, and move on with your evening.

A few tips for using the table:

- **Be honest about your time.** If you value a weekend at home, put a real figure next to your hours.

- **Think about how long you will stay.** If you plan to be in your home for years, the five-year view usually understates the long-term picture.

## What the Numbers Do Not Capture

Some of the biggest differences do not fit in a spreadsheet.

**No more ladders.** For many people, this is the whole decision, especially as knees start to complain.

**Invisible by day.** Our low-profile track is color-matched to blend with fascia, stucco or trim, so your home does not look like it is wearing holiday clips in July.

**Every holiday from one app.** Christmas Eve can glow in warm amber to complement luminarias and farolitos along the walk. Then New Year's, Valentine's Day and the rest of the calendar are a few taps away.

**Built for the high desert.** The system is designed for intense UV, spring wind season, and summer monsoon storms, which is exactly where seasonal strands tend to struggle.
